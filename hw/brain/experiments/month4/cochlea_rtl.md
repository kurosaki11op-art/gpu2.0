# The chip hears raw sound: integer cochlea and its RTL (15 Oct 2026, 08:30 IST)

- Model: `hw/brain/cochlea_int.py`. The 28 resonators of the month-1 cochlea with every multiply written as its
  shift-add terms, on integers with 8 fractional bits; frame sums of |y|; integer log picture. 91% of pixels identical
  to the float cochlea (50 clips; mean difference 0.7 of 255).
- RTL: `hw/rtl/cortex/cortex_cochlea.v` (+ `cochlea_coef.vh` generated by `gen_cochlea_coef.py`). One resonator at a
  time, ~3 cycles per sample: 1.35 M cycles per second of sound (50 ms at 27 MHz). No multiplier.
- Check: `run_check_cochlea.py`, 10 clips (one per word), every pixel: **0 mismatching clips** (`check_cochlea.log`).

Sound accuracy with the integer cochlea (exact integer chip model, month-2 rule, after 5 stages):

| Cochlea | 1 seed (`cochlea_int_check.log`) | 3 seeds (`cochlea_int_seeds.log`) |
|---|---|---|
| float (month 1-3) | 69.7 | 69.4 (69.0, 69.2, 70.0) |
| **integer (chip)** | 67.9 | **69.9** (69.9, 70.0, 69.8) |

SIMULATED. The single-seed drop was noise; over three seeds the chip's own cochlea is level with (slightly above) the
float one. Not yet connected to the encoder: sound codes use 10 channels and 64 detectors (23,040 bits, 422 KB of
memory), so the sound chip needs a wider encoder and the board's PSRAM or an ASIC.
