# Paper notes - novelty and out-of-distribution detection for old-versus-new decisions

Why this note: in staged learning the open problem we measured is old versus new. Frozen old-class scores separate old images from unseen-class images with only AUC 0.73 to 0.81 on MNIST (5 old classes versus 5 unseen), so new nodes absorb old images (MEASURED, L project). These four papers are the standard literature on that decision. Read from the arXiv PDFs on 25 Oct 2026 (ids verified by downloading https://arxiv.org/pdf/<id> and checking the title page). All paper numbers are reported by the authors, not reproduced by us. Anything not read is marked UNVERIFIED.

Papers read: (1) 1610.02136 Hendrycks and Gimpel, ICLR 2017 (all 12 pages). (2) 2010.03759 Liu, Wang, Owens, Li, NeurIPS 2020 (main text and appendix A and B). (3) 2110.06207 Vaze, Han, Vedaldi, Zisserman, ICLR 2022 (main text, appendices A to C and F read; D, E, G, H only skimmed, so UNVERIFIED in detail). (4) 2211.02633 Kim, Xiao, Konishi, Ke, Liu, "A Theoretical Study on Solving Continual Learning", NeurIPS 2022 (main text and appendix C and G; proofs A.1 to A.5 and appendices B, D, E, F, I, J not read, UNVERIFIED). Paper (4) is the one chosen for the "class-incremental learning with an explicit OOD head" slot.

## 1. arXiv 1610.02136 - A Baseline for Detecting Misclassified and Out-of-Distribution Examples (Hendrycks and Gimpel)

**(a) Mechanism.** Score = maximum softmax probability (MSP), S(x) = max_y p(y|x), p = softmax(logits). Higher means in-distribution; threshold it. Footnote 3: KL divergence of the softmax from uniform was also tried and gave highly correlated AUROC and AUPR, so it is entropy-like and adds nothing. Metrics: AUROC (threshold free, equals P(score_in > score_out)) and AUPR with either class positive. Section 4 adds an "abnormality module": a classifier plus an auxiliary decoder that reconstructs the input; the squared reconstruction error (reconstruction minus input, elementwise squared) is fed with the softmax vector to a small sigmoid network trained on clean examples versus noised ones (blur, rotation, Gaussian noise for MNIST; white/brown/pink noise for TIMIT). The module is trained with synthetic negatives, never with the real unseen classes.

**(b) Numbers (all AUROC, percent, MSP unless noted).**
- Misclassified-versus-correct (Table 1): MNIST 97 (test error 1.69), CIFAR-10 93 (error 4.96), CIFAR-100 87 (error 20.7). The text says the area degrades with test error. Mean MSP of wrong predictions is high (86, 80, 66), so MSP is a ranking signal, not a calibrated confidence.
- OOD (Table 2), MNIST in-distribution (3-layer 256-wide MLP, GELU, 30 epochs Adam): Omniglot 96, notMNIST 85, CIFAR-10bw 95, Gaussian 90, Uniform 99, all pooled 91. CIFAR-10 versus SUN 95, Gaussian 97; CIFAR-100 versus SUN 91, Gaussian 88.
- Text near-OOD (Table 6): 15 of 20 Newsgroups trained, 5 held out as unseen: AUROC 75 (the held-out subjects are topically close). Reuters 6 versus 2 unseen: 92. Reuters 40 versus 12 unseen: 95. POS tagging WSJ versus weblog (very close to training distribution): 61; WSJ versus Twitter 80.
- Abnormality module (Table 11), MNIST: softmax average 93 over five OOD sets, module 100 on all five (Omniglot 95 to 100, notMNIST 87 to 100, Gaussian 88 to 100). TIMIT (Table 10): softmax average 80, module 97.
- Dependence on closed-set accuracy and number of classes: the paper only shows the error-rate trend across datasets (above) and that the softmax signal was weak when the network was barely perturbed (TIMIT frame classifier with 29.69 percent error, clean versus distorted error 30.43 percent: softmax alone "did not provide useful" detection). There is NO experiment that varies the number of training classes. Every dataset used has at least 2 (IMDB), 6, 8, 10 or more classes. UNVERIFIED that anything holds at 5 classes.

**(c) Operations.** One softmax (exp, sum, divide) and a max. A counter version is immediate: the raw maximum class score or the margin top1 minus top2 needs only compare and subtract, no exp or log; ranking by MSP and by the raw score differ, and paper 3 shows the raw score is the better one (see below). No hyperparameters.

**(d) Limitations.** Softmax saturates: Gaussian noise gets 91 percent confidence on MNIST (intro). Weak on near-OOD (WSJ/weblog 61, 15/5 newsgroups 75). Authors call the 70 to 80 range "fair" and 60 to 70 "poor" (footnote 2). The abnormality module needs a decoder and trained negatives, and its 100 percent results come from synthetic-noise-versus-clean style tests, not semantic novelty.

**(f-partial) Fit to L.** The MSP result is the same kind of number L already measured: a score from a classifier trained on old data only. It says AUROC 0.73 to 0.81 is typical for an untuned softmax-type score under near-OOD. The abnormality-module idea (train a separate normal-versus-abnormal head on synthetic negatives from the data itself) is the seed of what paper 4 does with rotations.

## 2. arXiv 2010.03759 - Energy-based Out-of-distribution Detection (Liu et al.)

**(a) Mechanism.** Free energy of the logits f(x) in R^K at temperature T:
E(x; f) = -T * log( sum_i exp(f_i(x) / T) ) (Eq. 4). OOD decision: g(x) = 1 (in-distribution) if -E(x; f) > tau (Eq. 7). Use T = 1: the temperature ablation (Fig. 4a) shows FPR95 gets worse as T grows from 1 to 1000. Relation to softmax (Section 3.2): log max_y p(y|x) = -E(x; f(x) - f_max(x)) = E(x; f) + f_max(x) at T = 1. So log MSP is the energy of the logits after subtracting the max logit; the shift removes the magnitude information (E is lower and f_max higher for in-distribution points, so the two partly cancel). Optional fine-tuning with auxiliary outlier data (needs real OOD images: 80 Million Tiny Images with CIFAR classes removed): loss = cross-entropy + lambda * ( E[max(0, E(x_in) - m_in)^2] + E[max(0, m_out - E(x_out))^2] ), lambda = 0.1, 10 epochs, lr 0.001 cosine, batch 128 in / 256 outlier. Margins searched over m_in in {-3,-5,-7} and m_out in {-15,-19,-23,-27} in the main text, but appendix B lists the optimum as "m_in -23 for CIFAR-10 and -27 for CIFAR-100, m_out -5" which has the roles swapped relative to the text; treat the exact margin values as UNVERIFIED/inconsistent in the paper.

**(b) Numbers (WideResNet, six OOD sets: Textures, SVHN, Places365, LSUN-Crop, LSUN-Resize, iSUN; no MNIST and no Fashion-MNIST in this paper).**
- CIFAR-10 pretrained (test error 5.16 percent), averaged over the six sets (Table 1/2): MSP FPR95 51.04, AUROC 90.90; energy FPR95 33.01, AUROC 91.88; ODIN 35.71 / 91.09; Mahalanobis 37.08 / 93.27. FPR95 falls by 18.03 points; AUROC gains only about 1 point.
- Per set (Table 4), AUROC MSP versus energy: iSUN 89.83 vs 92.62; Places365 88.20 vs 89.89; Texture 88.50 vs 85.22 (energy WORSE); SVHN 91.89 vs 90.96 (energy WORSE); LSUN-Crop 95.65 vs 98.35; LSUN-Resize 91.37 vs 94.24. So energy is not uniformly better on AUROC.
- CIFAR-100 pretrained (test error 24.04 percent): MSP FPR95 80.41, AUROC 75.53; energy 73.60 / 79.56; Mahalanobis 54.04 / 84.12. SVHN row: MSP 71.44 AUROC vs energy 73.99. These are the low-accuracy, many-class numbers: AUROC 0.75 to 0.80 is in the same range as L's 0.73 to 0.81 (INFERENCE: the range is common when the closed-set classifier is weak).
- Closed-set accuracy link: pretrained energy AUROC is 91.88 at 5.16 percent error and 79.56 at 24.04 percent error, i.e. it falls with closed-set error, though the dataset also changes (10 versus 100 classes). No controlled ablation of class count. SVHN in-distribution (Table 6, WideResNet): MSP AUROC 96.33, energy 97.18.
- With outlier-exposure fine-tuning: CIFAR-10 average FPR95 8.53 (Outlier Exposure) versus 3.32 (energy), AUROC 98.30 versus 98.92; CIFAR-100 FPR95 58.10 versus 47.55, AUROC 85.19 versus 88.46. Classification error stays similar (4.87 versus 5.16 percent CIFAR-10; 24.58 versus 24.04 percent CIFAR-100). Without real outliers (pretrained only) none of this applies.
- Comparison with generative hybrid models (Table 3, AUROC): CIFAR-10 in-distribution, energy pretrained 0.91 on SVHN, 0.87 on CIFAR-100, 0.78 on CelebA; energy fine-tuned 0.99, 0.94, 1.00.

**(c) Operations.** logsumexp over K logits: K exponentials, a sum, a log. At T = 1 it is the maximum logit plus log(1 + sum of exp(f_i - f_max)); the correction term is between 0 and log K. So max logit is the cheap limit of energy (INFERENCE from the formula; paper 3 uses exactly the max logit). For a counter chip with integer scores: a small lookup table of log2(1 + 2^-d) values for the gap d to the max score would give a bit-cheap energy (ESTIMATED design idea, not in the paper). Margin version: the fine-tune loss is itself a margin (hinge) on the energy, but it needs gradient training and real outlier data.

**(d) Limitations.** Tested only at 10 or 100 classes with strong WideResNets; no small-class-count study; no near-OOD semantic test (all OOD sets are different datasets, i.e. far-OOD or style shift). Energy sometimes loses to MSP on AUROC (Texture, SVHN). Fine-tuning needs auxiliary real outlier data, which L does not have in an exemplar-free stage. Mahalanobis, which uses class-conditional feature statistics, was better on CIFAR-100 (84.12 versus 79.56) but needs a covariance matrix.

**(f-partial) Fit to L.** Cheap to test: logsumexp of the old-class evidence counters versus max counter versus margin, on the same frozen scores. Expect a modest gain at best (about 1 AUROC point on the paper's own average; larger FPR95 gain).

## 3. arXiv 2110.06207 - Open-Set Recognition: a Good Closed-Set Classifier is All You Need? (Vaze et al.)

**(a) Mechanism.** Open-set recognition (OSR): after training on known classes C, return a score S(y in C | x) for "belongs to a known class". Baselines: MSP, S = max_y p(y|x); and the proposed maximum logit score (MLS), S = max_j w_j . Phi(x), the largest raw class score before softmax. Argument for MLS (appendix B): cross-entropy raises the feature norm of known-class samples more than of unseen-class samples ("hard" samples are pulled toward the origin), and softmax normalisation cancels that magnitude; feature norm alone ||Phi(x)|| also "performs remarkably well" as a score. The recipe to raise closed-set accuracy: longer training (600 epochs), cosine learning rate with restarts, RandAugment, label smoothing, warm-up, optional 5-model ensemble; backbone VGG32 (about 1 million parameters on MNIST, weight decay 1e-4, batch 128). Calibration argument (Eq. 1, perfect calibration P(y_hat = y | f(x) = p) = p): lower test error means better calibration, so low confidence flags new classes.

**(b) Numbers.**
- Correlation closed-set accuracy versus open-set AUROC: Pearson rho = 0.95 on the standard OSR benchmarks (Fig. 2; MSP, ARPL, ARPL+CS; MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, CIFAR+50, TinyImageNet; averaged over five known/unknown splits, individual splits shown too). TinyImageNet ablation of training tricks (Table 5): rho = 0.93; accuracy 64.3 to 85.3 and AUROC 68.9/69.6 to 84.0 (MLS) across rows. ImageNet (1000 known classes, 1000 unseen classes from ImageNet-21K-P): rho = 0.88 on the "Hard" split and 0.63 on "Easy" across architectures; within the ResNet family rho = 1.00 (Easy) and 0.99 (Hard). The ViT pre-trained on 21K classes breaks the trend. Out-of-distribution check: four ResNets on CIFAR-100 with CIFAR-10 as OOD, rho = 0.97 (appendix F). Caveat stated in appendix A: the trend "seems less clear at very high accuracies" and may weaken as accuracy saturates.
- Standard OSR benchmark, AUROC averaged over five splits (Table 1). MNIST and SVHN and CIFAR-10 use 6 known and 4 unknown classes; CIFAR+N uses 4 known classes and N = 10 or 50 unknown CIFAR-100 classes; TinyImageNet 20 known, 180 unknown.

| Method | MNIST | SVHN | CIFAR10 | CIFAR+10 | CIFAR+50 | TinyImageNet |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MSP baseline as previously reported | 97.8 | 88.6 | 67.7 | 81.6 | 80.5 | 57.7 |
| MSP+ (better training) | 98.6 | 96.0 | 90.1 | 95.6 | 94.0 | 82.7 |
| MLS (max logit, better training) | 99.3 | 97.1 | 93.6 | 97.9 | 96.5 | 83.0 |
| ARPL+CS, previous best | 99.7 | 96.7 | 91.0 | 97.1 | 95.1 | 78.2 |
| (ARPL+CS)+ with the same training | 99.2 | 96.8 | 93.9 | 98.1 | 96.7 | 82.5 |

  The previously reported baseline is weak because it was trained for 30 epochs only (appendix C). Average gain of MLS over the reported baseline: 15.6 AUROC points. MLS is 0.7 points better than the previous best on average; (ARPL+CS)+ and MLS differ by 0.03 points on average.
- The two numbers to compare with L: MNIST 6 known versus 4 unknown gives AUROC 0.986 to 0.993 for a trained VGG32. L's frozen old-class scores give 0.73 to 0.81 on 5 versus 5. The 5-versus-5 setting here is not directly measured by the paper (UNVERIFIED), but the MNIST gap is large enough that the cause is more likely L's closed-set quality or score, than an unavoidable limit of few classes (INFERENCE).
- Difficulty depends on which unseen classes (Semantic Shift Benchmark, Table 3, ResNet50, AUROC Easy / Hard): CUB MLS 88.3 / 79.3 (accuracy 86.2); Stanford Cars 94.0 / 82.2 (accuracy 97.1); FGVC-Aircraft 90.7 / 82.3 (accuracy 91.7); ImageNet 78.7 / 72.8 (accuracy 78.8). The Hard split costs 5 to 12 AUROC points; random subsampling of 10 times more unseen classes costs only about 0.6 points. So near-OOD similarity matters far more than the number of unseen classes. Out-of-distribution table (appendix F, CIFAR-100 trained): MLS 73.5 on Gaussian noise but 77.7 on CIFAR-10 (near-OOD, similar images): the authors say OSR and OOD "may have different, and possibly orthogonal, solutions".
- Large-scale OOD (Table 8, six sets averaged, AUROC): CIFAR-10 MSP 90.9, ODIN 91.1, energy 91.9, Mahalanobis 93.3, VOS 94.1, MLS 95.1; CIFAR-100 MSP 75.5, ODIN 77.4, energy 79.6, Mahalanobis 84.1, MLS 80.8 (the list there is copied from Liu et al.).

**(c) Operations.** MLS is a max over class logits: no exp, no log, no normalisation. It is the cheapest score of the four papers and is exactly "max of the class counters" for a counter chip. In a counter chip with integer evidence per class, the equivalent of MLS is the largest integer class evidence; its natural scale is the feature norm (total number of active features), which is also a counter (INFERENCE).

**(d) Limitations.** The conclusion is conditional: the claim is "insufficient evidence to reject" the title question. The accuracy-AUROC relation is correlational and loosens at high accuracy and when architectures differ. Datasets with 4 to 6 known classes (CIFAR+N, CIFAR-10 six known) have poorly defined "class" semantics; appendix G argues (as I read it, skimmed, so UNVERIFIED) that a classifier trained on few broad classes cannot learn what a "class" means, so detecting a new one is ill-posed; this is why the paper proposes fine-grained splits with 50 to 100 known classes. Hard splits lose 5 to 12 points. Large pre-training on the unseen classes breaks the trend (ViT).

**(f-partial) Fit to L.** Two claims transfer directly: (i) score with the raw maximum evidence, not a normalised probability; (ii) raise closed-set accuracy of the old classes before blaming the novelty rule. Both are testable on L's existing frozen scores with no new training.

## 4. arXiv 2211.02633 - A Theoretical Study on Solving Continual Learning (Kim et al.)

**(a) Mechanism.** Class-incremental learning (CIL) is decomposed (Eq. 1 and 2) as P(x in X_{k0,j0} | D) = P(x in X_{k0,j0} | x in X_{k0}, D) * P(x in X_{k0} | D): within-task prediction (WP, which is task-incremental learning) times task-id prediction (TP). Assumptions: classes inside a task have disjoint domains, tasks have disjoint domains. Cross-entropy for each part: H_WP, H_TP, H_CIL.
- Theorem 1: if H_TP(x) <= delta and H_WP(x) <= eps then H_CIL(x) <= eps + delta. Corollary 1 states the same in expectation.
- Theorem 2: TP and per-task OOD detection bound each other. (i) If H_TP <= delta, then using P'_k = P(x in X_k | D) as the OOD detector gives H_OOD,k <= delta for all k. (ii) If H_OOD,k <= delta_k for every task, then setting P(x in X_k | D) = P'_k / sum_k P'_k gives H_TP <= (sum_k 1[x in X_k] e^{delta_k}) * (sum_k (1 - e^{-delta_k})).
- Theorem 3: H_OOD,k <= delta_k and H_WP <= eps imply H_CIL <= eps + (sum_k 1[x in X_k] e^{delta_k}) * (sum_k (1 - e^{-delta_k})).
- Theorem 4 (necessity): if H_CIL(x) <= eta then there exist a WP with H_WP <= eta, a TP with H_TP <= eta and an OOD detector for each task with H_OOD,k <= eta. Contraposition: if no OOD detector (or TP, or WP) can reach loss eta, then H_CIL > eta.
- Theorem 5 (appendix C): the ordinary argmax over concatenated task outputs is a special case with OOD_k = sigmoid(max f(x)_k) and temperature tau_k near 0; so "max logit of each task head, then argmax across heads" is the TP rule. Other definitions: WP via softmax with temperature nu_k (Eq. 12), TP by normalising the per-task OOD probabilities with exponent 1/tau_k (Eq. 13).
- Practical methods: take a task-incremental method with no forgetting (HAT hard attention masks, or SupSup) and train each task with an OOD-strong learner. OOD methods used: ODIN (temperature scaling tau_k plus an input perturbation x~ = x - eps_k sign(-grad_x log s(x; tau_k)_y), Eq. 10 and 11) and CSI (contrastive learning with rotation-based synthetic negatives, 700 epochs, LARS lr 0.1, batch 128; hyperparameters in appendix G, HAT penalty lambda per task, etc.). Variants "+c" learn a per-task calibration alpha_k f(x)_k + beta_k from a small replay buffer (200 samples on MNIST and CIFAR-10, 2000 on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet); the variants without "+c" are exemplar-free.

**(b) Numbers.** Per-task AUC (task-k model scores task-k test data as in-distribution and other tasks' test data as OOD) averaged over tasks. Table 1 (ODIN on C100-10T): DER++ AUC 85.99 to 88.21 with CIL accuracy 53.71 to 55.29; Sup AUC 79.16 to 80.58 with CIL 44.58 to 46.74; ODIN lowered AUC for several methods and their CIL accuracy fell with it (OWM 28.91 to 28.88, PASS 33.00 to 31.00, BiC 52.92 to 48.65), LwF being the exception. Table 2 (AUC / CIL accuracy, same task models, ODIN versus CSI):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HAT + ODIN | 82.5 / 62.6 | 77.8 / 41.2 | 75.4 / 25.8 | 72.3 / 38.6 | 71.8 / 30.0 |
| HAT + CSI | 91.2 / 87.8 | 84.5 / 63.3 | 86.5 / 54.6 | 76.5 / 45.7 | 78.5 / 47.1 |
| Sup + ODIN | 82.4 / 62.6 | 80.6 / 46.7 | 81.6 / 36.4 | 74.0 / 41.1 | 74.6 / 36.5 |

So in the range AUC 0.72 to 0.83 the exemplar-free CIL accuracy is only 25 to 47 percent (and 62.6 on C10-5T with AUC 0.82), and at AUC 0.91 it rises to 86 to 88 percent on CIFAR-10 (2 classes per task). Final accuracy (Table 3, mean of 5 runs): M-5T HAT+CSI 94.4, HAT 81.9, Sup 70.1, Sup+CSI 80.7, DER++ (replay) 95.3, LwF 85.5; C10-5T HAT+CSI 87.8 versus DER++ 66.0 and iCaRL 63.4; C100-10T HAT+CSI 63.3, Sup+CSI 65.1 versus DER++ 53.7; T-5T HAT+CSI 45.7 versus Sup 41.8, BiC 41.7. Exemplar-free regularisation methods are far weaker: MUC 52.9 and PASS 47.3 on C10-5T, MUC 30.4 and PASS 33.0 on C100-10T. TIL (within-task) accuracy of HAT+CSI 99.9 on M-5T and 98.7 on C10-5T (Table 4). The paper does NOT report a 5-versus-5 MNIST old-versus-new AUC; M-5T has two classes per task. Run time: HAT+CSI 28.68 hours and Sup+CSI 18.41 hours on one RTX 3090 for C10-5T (appendix H); parameter counts in Table 8.

**(c) Operations.** The decomposition itself is a product of two probabilities and an argmax. The per-task OOD score used in the exemplar-free variants is the maximum logit of that task's head (through a sigmoid); the sigmoid is monotone so a counter chip can use the raw maximum. The expensive part is the OOD training (CSI contrastive learning with rotation augmentation, 700 epochs, ResNet-18), not inference. A counter or margin form exists by Theorem 5: argmax over tasks of max class evidence.

**(d) Limitations (the authors' own, plus what the experiments show).** Authors: the theory "gives no guidance on how" to build WP and TP/OOD, the two example methods are empirical, and an open question is the necessary condition for WP and TP to do well (Section 4.5). CSI makes training very slow. Task-id accuracy depends on the scale of independently trained heads (the "+c" calibration uses a replay buffer, so the exemplar-free versions are the ones without it, and the "+c" gain is about 0 to 3 points on CIFAR and Tiny-ImageNet, 2.5 points for HAT on M-5T). Sup+CSI is weak on MNIST (80.7 on M-5T versus 94.4 for HAT+CSI). All tasks have 2 to 40 classes; no experiment has 5 old classes versus 5 unseen. On Tiny-ImageNet AUC is only 0.77 to 0.79 even with CSI, and CIL accuracy 46 to 49 percent: a near-OOD ceiling again.

**(e) What the decomposition says.** Good CIL requires both (1) good within-task prediction and (2) good task-id prediction, equivalently good OOD detection for each task against everything that is not that task (Theorem 4 gives necessity; Theorems 1 and 3 give sufficiency). Regardless of the algorithm, a CIL model that cannot tell old from new cannot be accurate. With no exemplars: the OOD detector for task k can use only task k's data. The paper argues replay data act as OOD data for the current task, and that regularisation-only exemplar-free methods have weak TP "since the training of each task does not consider OOD detection", so inter-task class separation fails (Section 4.5). Hence exemplar-free class-IL is possible in the theorem's sense only if each task's own model contains a good novelty detector learned from its own data (synthetic negatives such as rotations in CSI, or another self-supervised signal); the paper demonstrates that this works on CIFAR-10 (87.8 percent exemplar-free, above replay methods at 66.0), but with AUC 0.72 to 0.83 it does not (25 to 47 percent). The theory is an existence/necessity result, not a proof that exemplar-free class-IL is achievable: it says nothing about whether the OOD detector can be learned from one task's data alone. For L: the measured old-versus-new AUC of 0.73 to 0.81 is the binding quantity, and by Theorem 4 no node-allocation rule can beat what that detector allows (INFERENCE from the theorem, not a quantified bound; the bound in Theorem 3 uses cross-entropy of a calibrated probability, which we have not computed for L).

## Synthesis for L

**How good can old-versus-new get with few base classes?** From these papers: with a well-trained closed-set classifier and the max logit, AUROC 0.986 to 0.993 on MNIST with 6 known and 4 unseen classes, 0.936 on CIFAR-10 6 versus 4, 0.979 on CIFAR+10 (4 known classes, Vaze Table 1, trained 600 epochs, MEASURED by the authors). With plain MSP and weak training the same benchmark gives 0.978 (MNIST), 0.677 (CIFAR-10). Near-OOD costs 5 to 12 AUROC points (SSB Hard versus Easy). Weak closed-set classifiers sit at 0.72 to 0.80 (CIFAR-100 energy 0.7956, Tiny-ImageNet 0.77 with CSI). L's 0.73 to 0.81 therefore looks like a weak-closed-set-classifier number, not a ceiling of the task (INFERENCE, to be tested). No paper here measures 5 classes versus 5 unseen on Fashion-MNIST, and none varies the number of training classes in a controlled way.

**The score ranking, from the papers:** raw maximum evidence (MLS) at least as good as energy, which is at least as good as MSP on AUROC, with energy's FPR95 gain larger than its AUROC gain. Real unseen-style data (outlier exposure) is the biggest single lever (CIFAR-10 FPR95 51 to 3.3) but exemplar-free forbids real outliers; synthetic negatives (rotation, noise, blur, cutout) are the exemplar-free substitute.

**One concrete small test (counter-compatible, no new training).** On the existing MNIST 5-old versus 5-unseen frozen model (and the same on Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10 splits if available), compute AUROC of old-versus-unseen for five scores computed from the same stored class counters: (1) max class evidence (MLS), (2) top1 minus top2 margin, (3) the L softmax-like normalised score currently used, (4) logsumexp of class evidence via the log2(1+2^-d) table, (5) total active-feature count (feature norm analogue, Vaze appendix B). Repeat at 3 closed-set accuracy levels (vary the base training length or sample count) and plot AUROC against old-class accuracy to see whether L follows the rho 0.95 line. Success criterion: any score reaching AUROC at or above 0.90 on MNIST 5-versus-5 would put old-versus-new within the range of the literature; if all five stay near 0.75 then the cause is the representation and the next step is a self-supervised negative (rotation of the stored sample as pseudo-unseen) per Kim et al. All numbers from this test will be labelled MEASURED when run; none exist yet.
