# Questions the judges are likely to ask, with honest answers

Every answer keeps the label (SIMULATED / ESTIMATED / MEASURED). Source of each number: [claims sheet](cortex_claims_sheet.md), [results at a glance](results_at_a_glance.md).

**1. Has it run on real hardware?** No. The design fits a Tang Nano 20K in place and route (SIMULATED) and the RTL is bit-exact with the model, but the board run is the next milestone and gives the first MEASURED numbers.

**2. Is this the first chip that learns continuously?** No. Hyperdimensional-computing accelerators and Intel Loihi 2 exist ([prior art](prior_art_cortex.md)). What is new is the combination: dreams generated from 10-bit counters (no stored examples), a night that ends by itself, no multiplier, bit-exact from raw senses to the decision. In our own test after 5 stages we are ahead of HDC on digits by 2.2 and on sound by 5.7 points (SIMULATED).

**3. Does it match a neural network?** Learning everything at once, with the slow-table option (ASIC; 5 seeds, LOOK AGAIN, SIMULATED): digits 98.4 vs 97.8, clothing 88.3 vs 87.3, words 87.5 vs 85.5 for a small MLP on the same input: level, not better. A floating-point MLP we trained on raw clothing pixels reached 88.4 and a small CNN 89.2 (words: CNN 91.4). Without the slow table the network is ahead on clothing (84.8 vs 87.3). The chip's advantage is learning in stages: with the slow-table option digits 95.9, clothing 81.4, words 83.6 (95.8 / 78.3 / 82.9 without it), vs a network that forgets (19.5 / 20.0 / 15.9).

**3b. What limited the scores, and what did you do about it?** A floating-point classifier trained on the chip's own code reaches 87.9% on clothing and 98.3% on digits, so the senses are not the limit; the one-pass 5-bit readout is. A slow second table that follows the fast one (shifts and adds, bit-exact in RTL, ASIC option, +1.6x table memory) gives clothing 83.4 to 87.6, words 83.1 to 86.4, digits 97.0 to 97.8 (single view), and staged clothing 78.4 to 81.6 when it follows slowly (consolidation lag damps the bias toward the newest classes). A finer detector grid for images adds a little more all at once but made staged digits worse in an earlier test, so it is an option.

**4. Does it really never forget?** No, do not say that. A new condition of known classes (rotated digits) dropped upright digits from 95 to 45%; a frozen dream memory keeps 85% and is an ASIC build option in the RTL (SIMULATED).

**5. Where did it fail, and what did you try?** Staged learning is limited by dream fidelity (the last-stage classes absorb older ones); cheap fixes (more daydreams, margins, structured dreams, bias rules) failed; only prototype dreams help (+2 on clothing for 4x dream memory). Spoken words from 1 to 5 recordings stay weak (12 to 29% on the new words). Also rejected: a second sensory layer, expansion coding, onset channels, tempo and pitch normalisation, wider-in-time sound detectors (about 2 points worse), a shared prototype pool (no memory saving), table rebuilds and merge variants. Kept: IMPRINT + short sleep, word alignment, LOOK AGAIN, margin 32. Failures are in the [journal](journal.md).

**6. What does it cost?** Images: 84 KB memory. Sound: 422 KB (needs PSRAM or an ASIC). The Tang Nano uses 89% logic, all 46 block RAMs and 0 multipliers (SIMULATED place and route). SKY130: logic under 1 mm2, 18.8 mm2 with open SRAM macros (ESTIMATED).

**7. How much energy does it save?** About 74x per decision counting only the operations needed (9.4 nJ vs 696 nJ against an 8-bit network, ESTIMATED). The SKY130 layout estimate is 0.18 to 0.26 uJ per decision, 20 to 28x above the operation count. Say "per operation needed", not "per chip".

**8. Why no multiplier or backprop?** The brain principle we committed to. Learning is counting into a 5-bit table and 10-bit counters; dreams are rejection sampling from counters; the sense layer is XNOR plus popcount and winner-take-all.

**9. What are dreams, in one sentence?** While idle, the chip draws pretend examples of old classes from its counters and learns them again, so new learning does not overwrite old knowledge, and it stops by itself when the table stops being surprised.

**10. How does one chip give knowledge to another?** Because memory is counts: merge two chips by adding counters (91.4% on all ten digits after one night in the board-demo emulator), or hand over one memory and keep learning (95.6 / 82.7 / 71.6, SIMULATED).

**11. Why does this matter (scope)?** Less retraining means less energy, less heat and less discarded hardware; weight transfer without a server; learning in the field. It is a small continual learner for sensors (images, sound), not a language model, and it does not replace GPUs.

**12. What is the layout status?** SKY130 open-source flow, detailed routing at 1 remaining violation at a memory-macro edge (from 174,309 on the first pass), ESTIMATED, no sign-off, no fabrication planned yet.

**13. What will you do with more time or money?** First the board run (MEASURED numbers), then learned word features and higher-fidelity dreams for clothing, then write-up and demo video; horizon 6 to 8 months, then shrink the design and look for investors.

**14. Is the data realistic?** Standard digit and clothing image sets and a small spoken-word set (10 words). Results on larger vocabularies or real noise are not tested; say so.

**15. Does it really learn like a brain?** Brain-inspired, not brain-identical, and we say which parts are engineered ([audit](brain_principles_audit.md)). Brain-like and measured (SIMULATED): local counting rules with no backprop or multipliers, sparse competitive detectors that now learn on the chip, replay and sleep that the chip triggers from its own surprise count, a slow consolidating table, weak-synapse pruning, and learning from few labels on digits (5% labels 95.0 vs 95.5 fully labelled). Engineered: the cochlea and the word-alignment window (an echoic buffer: the chip holds one second and attends to its loudest part; a causal onset trigger was about 3 points worse), labels for naming classes, and the host scheduling.

**16. What is your newest result?** Changing three settings (update margin 256, 1,500 dreams per old class, one night pass) with no extra memory: digits learned in stages 94.1 -> 95.5, clothing 80.8 -> 83.3, spoken words clean 68.5 -> 73.35 (SIMULATED, 3 seeds, RTL bit-exact at margin 256). Training on noisy copies of the clips also raised word accuracy at 5 dB from 33.5 to 56.4 (a control with the same clean clips twice gives 36.8) ([redesign tests](../hw/results/brain_strict_redesign.md)).

**17. What did not work, and what is still open?** Tried and failed: an unsupervised category layer, cascade synapses, dreams with lateral inhibition, a second sensory layer (twice), novelty recruitment, a causal onset trigger, learning new words with time-shifted views, and more detectors (no gain for the memory). Still open: staged learning is below all-at-once (clothing 83.3 vs 86.6), new spoken words from one recording stay weak, the chip does not reach a CNN's accuracy on clothing, and few labels on clothing (about 79 vs 83.3 at 10% labels) is limited by how few labels there are. Say these plainly; each is in the redesign tests note with its numbers.
**18a. Newest numbers on the weak spots (20 Oct night, SIMULATED):** (i) the two-sense result holds on a second, noisier sound set (Speech Commands digits): zero picture labels 92.3, **96.9 after sleep cleaning** vs 97.4 with all labels; (ii) clothing with a name source that is only 60-73 % right reaches **85.2-85.4** after cleaning vs 86.55 with every label (the ear is simulated; no spoken clothing set); (iii) a second sense raises staged digit accuracy by +1.1 to +2.2 (96.6 / 97.7 vs 95.5); (iv) new spoken words from 1-5 recordings are within about 2-5 points of a GPU-style model (50.2 / 56.4 / 62.3 vs 55.1 / 63.7 at 1 / 5); (v) the word window is aligned inside a one-second echoic buffer (like the brain's sensory memory); a fully streaming trigger would cost about 3 points at 35 words. Still true: staged clothing is about 3 points below its one-pass 86.55.

**18e. Newest staged gain (21 Oct, SIMULATED):** a surprise-tagged tail memory (a second count memory kept only for examples that made the table write; 3/16 of dreams drawn from it) lifts staged clothing 83.2 to 84.5 and digits 94.9 to 95.9 (5 seeds) and words 73.35 to 74.15 (20) and 68.29 to 68.53 (35); costs about +57 KB; not in RTL yet; the newest words at 35 words lose 3.8 points.

**18f. Damaged input (21 Oct, SIMULATED):** weighting each sense by how confident it is (powers of two, shifts only) lifts picture-25%-damaged + sound from 97.4 to 98.4 and clean two-sense from 98.3 to 98.9; it loses 0.35 when the sound is the damaged one.

**18g. Second look for words (21 Oct, SIMULATED):** a second table on a finer sound code, consulted for the 20-40 % least-sure clips, lifts staged words 73.35 to 75.4-75.9 (20 words) and 68.29 to 70.7 (35 words) against a CNN ruler of 77.05 / about 72.5; about 2x sound memory, ASIC option; decision logic in RTL, bit-exact.

**18h. Noise (21 Oct, SIMULATED):** one noisy copy of every training clip learned after the clean one (no extra memory, double training examples) lifts staged words at 5 dB from 39.5 to 59.0 (20 words) and from 27.4 to 42.8 (35 words), and clean from 73.35 to 73.59 / 68.29 to 69.83; the newest words also rise.

**18i. Words, newest (21 Oct, SIMULATED):** with a second look and noisy-copy training together, staged words reach 75.7 clean at 20 words (CNN ruler 77.05) and 72.4 at 35 words (CNN about 72.5, a tie), and 5 dB noise goes from 27.4 to 47.2 at 35 words; about 2x sound memory, double the training examples; not a tie at 20 words.

**18k. Strong or weak with 60,000 images? (22 Oct, CNN MEASURED, chip SIMULATED)** All together: CNN 90.96 clothing / 98.87 digits, chip 87.63 / 97.92 (3.3 and 0.95 behind). In stages with no stored examples: chip 81.0 / 95.6 (follow interval scaled with the stage size), CNN 19.95 / 19.50. Say: far ahead of a network when learning in stages, a few points behind it when all the data is available at once.

**18l. Second look at 60,000 images (22 Oct, SIMULATED):** clothing one pass 87.58 -> 88.93 (CNN 90.96), digits 97.92 -> 98.45 (CNN 98.87); staged clothing 81.0 -> 82.5; about 2.8x table memory and 40 % more reads.

**18j. What happens with more data? (22 Oct, SIMULATED)** One pass barely improves (clothing 86.55 -> 87.31, digits 97.47 -> 97.88 with 60,000 images). Staged learning gets worse as each stage gets bigger (clothing 83.3 -> 78.0, digits 95.5 -> 93.4 at 60,000 images; settings do not fix it): our staged numbers are for small stages of 2,000 images; say so.

**18d. How close are you to a trained network on one pass?** (same 10,000 training images, SIMULATED chip, MEASURED CNN) With a "second look" (a second table on a finer code consulted only for the 20 % of inputs the first table is least sure about) clothing reaches **88.0** against the CNN's 88.8, digits 98.1 against 98.55, with 41 % more reads and about 2.8x the memory (an ASIC option, not in RTL yet); staged clothing also improves slightly (83.7). New spoken words learned while their object is seen reach 56 / 71 from sound alone against 10 / 34 from 5 recordings.

**18c. Can it notice that something is new?** With two senses, yes (SIMULATED, 2 seeds): the chip knows digits 0-7; in an unlabelled stream, pairs of pictures and sounds it scores low on (AUROC 0.96 / 0.97) are flagged, 99 % of the flagged pairs really are new, they form groups, a handful of names label the groups, sleep cleans them and the chip learns the new digits without forgetting the old (all 10 digits 93.7 / 97.3 against 96.8 / 97.6 with every label given). Picture bits alone cannot tell (AUROC 0.53-0.57): the two senses together do it. Digits only; grouping runs on the host.

**18b. Can it form categories by itself?** With two senses, yes (SIMULATED, 2 seeds): pictures and sounds of the same digit occur together; 30 groups form with **no labels** (competitive learning on the joined code), 30 names in total (3 per class) label them, and sleep cleans the names. A picture learner then scores **96.1 / 97.0** on two dataset pairs against 97.4 with all 10,000 labels. Picture-only groups reach only about 67, so say "the second sense makes the category layer work". Caveats: digits only, the grouping loop runs on the host, a few seconds-to-minutes of software clustering.

**18. Does it use more than one sense, and does that help?** Yes (SIMULATED, bit-exact in RTL): a retina and a cochlea feed one table. Both senses together score 98.97 on digits (picture alone 97.4); when a quarter of the picture is damaged the sound brings 85 back to 97. A heard word can **name pictures with no picture labels**: the ear is right on only 73 % of unfamiliar speakers, the picture learner reaches 89.7 from its names and 93.9 after sleep-time neighbour cleaning (a small CNN given the same names: 83.5, and 82.9 after self-training; MEASURED ruler). Caveats: one dataset pair, the pooling runs on the host, nothing on silicon yet. See [sensory node](../hw/results/sensory_node.md).

**19. How does it compare with a GPU network on the same data?** A small CNN is only a ruler, never in the chip. Same 10,000 training images (MEASURED): all classes together CNN clothing 88.8 / digits 98.55 / 20 words 77.05 against L one pass 86.55 / 97.47 / about 75. **Learning in stages with no stored examples**: CNN 20 % without help, 28-32 % with a replay buffer the size of our memory, 88 % / 96 % only after storing about 4,000 images (24x our memory); L 83.3 / 95.5 with no stored examples (the learned table and the per-bit counts of the dream memory are kept; no examples). Say "no stored examples", not "no weights". Honest gaps: one-pass accuracy 1-4 points behind; staged clothing 3 points below one pass.

**19. What is wrong with the transformer and the GPU?** Nothing for their own job (training, big batches). For a device: the model is frozen after training, every generated token reads every weight while attention memory grows with the text, and a GPU is efficient only in a crowd: on the RTX 3050 one decision costs 69 mJ alone and 1.95 mJ in a batch of 1,024 (MEASURED, 35 times). Heat, cooling water and hardware turnover are motivation, not measured.

**20. Why should we use L instead?** For one job: learn on the device, small memory, one input at a time. Staged learning on 59,063 images: chip 95.5% (SIMULATED), CNN 19.6% (MEASURED); 84 KB, no multiplier; about 74 times less energy per decision by operation count (ESTIMATED); 41 of 41 bit-exact checks. The CNN is ahead when all is learned at once (99.09 vs 97.91). Keep the GPU for training and big models.

**21. Can real AI models run on L?** Three routes, status stated: alter an existing model (ternary models turn multiplies into adds; published at billions of parameters, but still a matrix of adds), design our own counting language model (counting classifier SIMULATED; language model is the next experiment), grow the memory (outside DRAM, sparse lookups; test quality against table size first). We claim the route and the tests that can prove it wrong, not a billion-parameter result.

**22. How did you test it, and did you run real AI models?** Standard tasks (handwritten digits, clothing images, spoken digits and words) on the integer model of the chip, which is exactly its arithmetic: all at once, in stages, with noise, with two senses, a new class from one example, two chips merged; 3 to 5 seeds, a held-out speaker (SIMULATED). A CNN and a small network on the same data, the CNN run on the RTX 3050 laptop GPU (MEASURED). The Verilog matches the model bit for bit in 41 of 41 checks, the real-cell netlist matches, a formal equivalence check passed (SIMULATED). Not tested: a language model or any large model on the chip, a board run, real-world noise, big vocabularies; the models run are classifiers and the CNN is only the ruler.
